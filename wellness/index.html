The four pillars

🧂 Sodium down

Target under 2,300mg/day. Lower sodium is linked to slower cyst growth and better blood pressure control in PKD.

💧 Fluid up

Build gradually toward ~3L/day. Higher fluid intake suppresses vasopressin, a hormone tied to cyst growth.

🚶 Movement, low-impact

Regular low-impact cardio + light resistance, avoiding contact sports and direct blows to the back/flank.

🌱 Plants first

5+ servings of fruit/veg daily, plant protein (beans, lentils, tofu) favored over red and processed meat.

How the ramp works

Six weeks, starting Aug 24. Each week nudges diet and exercise up slightly rather than overhauling everything at once — the goal is something you can actually sustain, not a crash reset. Use the Food Tracker tab to log meals against your sodium, protein, and fluid targets day to day.

6-week ramp (starts Mon, Aug 24)

PKD-specific caution: avoid contact/collision sports and activities with a direct risk of a blow to the back or flank (football, rugby, hockey, boxing/kickboxing, horseback riding) — enlarged kidneys are more vulnerable to cyst rupture. Walking, swimming, cycling, and the elliptical are all good low-impact options.
Medication caution: amlodipine is going up to 10mg and Lexapro is brand new — both can cause dizziness or lightheadedness in the first couple of weeks, and exercise-induced vasodilation can compound that. Rise slowly from sitting/lying, keep the first two weeks genuinely easy, and stop if you feel lightheaded.

What's actually changing, and what to watch

Amlodipine 5mg → 10mg (change)

  • Higher dose raises the chance of dizziness, flushing, headache, or ankle swelling, especially in the first 1–2 weeks.
  • Rise slowly from sitting or lying down — this matters more now since you're also on lisinopril and amitriptyline long-term, both of which can independently cause dizziness on standing. The dose bump adds to an effect your body may already be managing.
  • Avoid grapefruit/grapefruit juice entirely — it can raise amlodipine levels.
  • Alcohol adds to the blood-pressure-lowering effect — go easy, especially early on.

Starting Lexapro (escitalopram) (new)

  • Common early side effects — nausea, headache, dizziness, sleep changes, sweating — usually ease within 2–4 weeks as your body adjusts.
  • Exercise is generally fine and can help mood, but keep the first two weeks light while you see how you respond.
  • Small hyponatremia risk (see fluid note in Diet Guide) — worth a mention at your next lab check given the high-fluid PKD protocol.
  • The one real new interaction to know: adding Lexapro on top of your long-term amitriptyline raises a modest serotonin syndrome risk (both are serotonergic). This is a combination prescribers do use together deliberately, so it's likely already accounted for — but it's worth confirming with whoever prescribed the Lexapro that they knew about the amitriptyline. Watch for agitation, tremor, sweating, fast heart rate, or confusion in the first couple weeks, especially as the Lexapro dose is titrated.

Your existing meds — context, not changes

You've been stable on these for years, so no new monitoring is needed just because of this plan — but they're relevant background for the diet and exercise guidance above.

  • Lisinopril 40mg: ACE inhibitors can raise blood potassium, more so with reduced kidney function — not a concern at your current stage, but a good reason to keep potassium on the regular lab panel over time. Also: avoid NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen) — combined with an ACE inhibitor they raise both potassium and acute kidney injury risk; acetaminophen is the safer option for pain.
  • Amitriptyline 25mg: can cause dry mouth, constipation, and dizziness on standing — the dry mouth may actually make the 3L/day fluid goal easier to hit, and extra fluid helps counter the constipation too. If it's dosed at night for sleep, morning grogginess is common, so consider saving harder workouts for later in the day.
Bottom line: the amlodipine increase and the new Lexapro are the two things to actually monitor closely. Both compound existing effects from lisinopril and amitriptyline rather than introducing brand-new risk — mainly more dizziness-on-standing potential, and (with Lexapro specifically) a bit more serotonergic load on top of the amitriptyline. Worth a quick confirmation with your prescriber that they have the full medication list before Aug 24.
